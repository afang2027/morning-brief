South Carolina Insurance Bill Targets Free Windshield Replacements

South Carolina’s House Bill 4817 — the Insurance Rate Reduction and Policyholder Protection Act — has moved to the Senate Banking and Insurance Committee after passing the House, with provisions that would end the state’s 37-year-old mandatory zero-deductible windshield replacement benefit starting January 1, 2027. Under the bill, insurers must offer a zero-deductible option as a choice (not a mandate), and any applicable deductible would be capped at $100. The bill also expands the Department of Insurance’s fraud oversight authority and raises the property casualty excess premium tax credit from $1,250 to $3,000.

UN Trade and Development
Global Trade Update (May 2026): Invisible Barriers — The Costs of Non-Tariff Measures

UNCTAD’s May 2026 Global Trade Update finds that non-tariff measures (NTMs) — product regulations, mandatory standards, and certification requirements — now impose higher export costs than tariffs for 88% of countries, with the hidden cost equivalent to a 28% tariff when transparency is lacking. Least-developed countries lose roughly 10% of their G20 export volume simply from inability to comply with NTM requirements at the same level as other developing economies. For businesses navigating global supply chains, the report highlights that regulatory compliance strategy now matters as much as tariff management in trade planning.

New California and Texas Insurance Laws Taking Effect in 2026

Several major state insurance law changes are now in force for 2026: California’s Disaster Recovery Reform Act (SB 876) requires insurers to file disaster claims-handling plans and doubles penalties during declared emergencies, while Texas HB 2067 mandates quarterly reports on declined, canceled, or non-renewed policies. Louisiana now requires prominent prior-year premium display at renewal, and Georgia’s SB 69 restricts third-party litigation financing. These coordinated state-level moves reflect a national push for greater insurer accountability and rate transparency.

Art of the Day
Nighthawks
Edward Hopper (American, 1882–1967)
1942
Oil on canvas

Rendered in oil on canvas in 1942, this Modernist work depicts an urban nocturnal scene — silhouetted diners at a late-night counter viewed through plate glass from the empty street outside. Hopper’s composition balances architectural space with isolated human presence, drawing on subjects of dining and city life to distill a distinctly American mood of solitude amid society.

American Gothic
Grant Wood (American, 1891–1942)
1930
Oil on Beaver Board

Executed in oil on Beaver Board in 1930, this Realist-Modernist work presents two figures rooted in agricultural life — their postures, period fashion, and architectural backdrop rendered with exacting precision. Wood’s treatment of farm life and rural portraiture constructs a concentrated image of American regional identity during the Depression era.

Autumn
Wilson H. Irvine (American, 1869–1936)
c. 1914
Oil on canvas

Painted in oil on canvas around 1914 and explicitly classified within the American Scene and Impressionism styles, this work brings a broken-brushwork sensibility to the American landscape tradition. Irvine’s autumnal subject reflects early-twentieth-century interest in regional scenery as a vehicle for national identity and seasonal feeling.

Croquet Scene
Winslow Homer (American, 1836–1910)
1866
Oil on canvas

Rendered in oil on canvas in 1866, this Impressionist-Realist work by Winslow Homer captures fashionably dressed men and women at leisure on a croquet lawn. Homer’s attention to fabric, figure, and open-air light places the subjects within the genteel leisure culture of post-Civil War America, using genre subjects — fashion, leisure, social ritual — to observe with a reportorial eye.
